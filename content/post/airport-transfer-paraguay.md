Getting your airport transfer Paraguay booking sorted before you fly saves a lot of hassle on arrival day. Landing in Paraguay for the first time, most visitors fly into Silvio Pettirossi International Airport (AGT), about 15 kilometres northeast of downtown Asunción in the city of Luque. It is a small, manageable airport, nothing like the sprawl of Buenos Aires' Ezeiza or São Paulo's Guarulhos, but that also means it has fewer of the conveniences bigger hubs take for granted: no train link into the city, a handful of official taxi and transfer counters, and rideshare apps that work but aren't always the smoothest option straight off a long flight. Arranging an airport transfer in Paraguay before you land takes one variable off the table on a day when you're already tired, possibly disoriented by the heat, and carrying luggage.

## What arrivals at Silvio Pettirossi actually look like

After immigration and baggage claim, you'll walk out into a single arrivals hall. There is usually a cluster of drivers holding name signs, some from hotels, some from tour operators, some independent. Official airport taxis operate from a marked stand just outside the doors; agree the fare (or confirm it's metered) before you get in. It's a straightforward system, but if you land late at night, don't speak Spanish or Guaraní, and have never seen the layout of Asunción, having a driver already booked and waiting with your name on a sign removes a lot of the friction.

The drive into the city centre or the Villa Morra/Recoleta area, where most visitors and business travellers end up staying, typically takes 20 to 35 minutes depending on traffic and your exact destination. Asunción's road network is fairly simple to navigate: a few main avenues, among them Aviadores del Chaco, Avenida España and Avenida Mariscal López, carry most of the traffic between the airport corridor and the city.

## Airport transfer Paraguay options: transfer vs taxi vs rideshare

For a first-time visitor, the choice usually comes down to three options, each with real trade-offs:

- **A pre-booked airport transfer**: a driver meets you at arrivals, already knows your accommodation, and the price is fixed in advance. Best for late-night arrivals, families with luggage, or anyone who wants zero decisions to make after a long flight.
- **The official taxi stand**: reliable and regulated, but you'll want to confirm the fare or that the meter is running before departing, and drivers may not speak much English.
- **Rideshare apps**: available in Asunción and generally work well, though pickup points at the airport can be less obvious than at a curbside taxi rank, and connectivity issues on a foreign SIM can complicate things right when you land.

None of these is objectively wrong. A transfer simply trades a small premium for certainty, which matters more on a first trip, on a red-eye arrival, or if you're heading straight out to somewhere like [San Bernardino](/san-bernardino/) or another destination outside the capital rather than staying in Asunción itself.

## Practical information

- **Airport:** Silvio Pettirossi International Airport (AGT), Luque, about 15 km from central Asunción
- **Typical drive time to the centre:** 20 to 35 minutes, longer at rush hour
- **Currency:** bring some Paraguayan guaraníes or US dollars for small cash needs; check current exchange rates before you fly, as they move
- **What to bring:** your accommodation address written out (helps with drivers who don't read English), a charged phone, and light layers, since Paraguay's heat and humidity can be intense stepping off an air-conditioned flight
- **Best time to arrange a transfer:** book before you fly rather than on arrival, especially for late-night or early-morning flights when the arrivals hall is quieter and fewer independent drivers are around
- **SIM cards:** a local SIM or eSIM at the airport can save connectivity headaches for the ride in and the rest of your trip

## Getting your onward trip right

If Asunción is just your entry point and you're heading on to somewhere like Ciudad del Este for the [Iguazu Falls](/iguazu-falls-from-asuncion/) or south towards the Jesuit missions, it's worth thinking about that leg before you land too. A transfer straight from the airport to the [bus terminal](/asuncion-bus-terminal/) can save a stopover in the city if your onward connection leaves the same day. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to take a taxi from Silvio Pettirossi airport?

Yes, using the official taxi stand just outside arrivals is generally considered safe and is what most locals and business travellers do. The main thing to watch is agreeing the fare or confirming the meter is running before you set off, since unmarked or unofficial cars sometimes approach arriving passengers.

### How far in advance should I book an airport transfer in Paraguay?

Booking a day or two before you fly is usually enough, though for a late-night arrival or during a busy period such as Semana Santa it's worth arranging it further ahead so a driver is confirmed and waiting rather than something you're coordinating on landing.

### Does the airport have a train or metro connection into Asunción?

No. Silvio Pettirossi has no rail link, so road transport, whether taxi, private transfer, or rideshare, is the only way into the city. This is one of the main reasons a pre-arranged transfer is popular with first-time visitors.

### What should I do if my flight arrives very late at night?

This is exactly the situation where a pre-booked transfer earns its cost. Drivers for arranged transfers typically track your flight and wait even if it's delayed, whereas the taxi rank and rideshare pickup points can be quieter and less predictable in the small hours.

### Can I get a transfer directly to San Bernardino or another town outside Asunción?

Yes, most transfer operators will run you further afield than the city centre for an adjusted price, so specify your final destination when you book so the driver and vehicle are the right fit for the distance.
