Asunción doesn't announce itself the way some South American capitals do. There's no single
postcard landmark pulling every visitor toward it, and the city rewards a slower, more curious
approach instead. Figuring out what to do in Asunción mostly comes down to accepting that it's a
lived-in capital first and a tourist destination second, and then leaning into that: a walk through
the old government quarter, an evening on the riverside Costanera, a long lunch in Villa Morra, and
a morning lost in the noise of Mercado 4. None of it is spectacular in isolation. Together it adds
up to a genuinely good few days.

## What to do in Asunción: start in the historic centre

The old centre, around the Palacio de los López (the presidential palace) and Plaza de la
Independencia, is where Asunción's history is most visible. The palace itself, an ornate neoclassical
building overlooking the river, isn't generally open for public tours, but it's worth walking past
in the late afternoon when the light catches its facade. Nearby, the Panteón Nacional de los Héroes
holds the remains of several figures from Paraguay's independence and later wars, and functions as a
kind of national mausoleum in miniature. The surrounding streets have a faded, unhurried grandeur,
some buildings beautifully kept, others visibly waiting for restoration, that gives the centre more
character than its size would suggest. For deeper background on the country these buildings sit in,
see the [Asunción Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n) and our broader
[essential guide to Paraguay](/essential-guide-paraguay/).

## Walk the Costanera at sunset

Asunción's Costanera, a long riverside promenade along the Río Paraguay, is one of the best free
things to do in the city. It's especially good in the hour before sunset, when the heat breaks a
little and locals come out to walk, cycle and drink tereré (cold yerba mate) on the grass. The
promenade also gives a clear view across the river toward the Chaco, a useful reminder of how close
that very different landscape sits to the capital. It's an easy, no-planning-required way to spend
an evening and understand how Asunción actually lives day to day, away from the museums and
monuments.

## Eat and shop in Villa Morra and beyond

Villa Morra and the neighbouring Recoleta area hold most of Asunción's better restaurants, cafés and
food parks, and are worth a dedicated evening or two rather than a rushed stop. Our
[Villa Morra food park](/villa-morra-food-park/) guide covers the casual food-truck scene there in
detail. For a more market-driven, less polished side of the city, Mercado 4 is the place to see
Asunción's everyday commerce up close, from produce to leather goods to yerba mate gear. Our
[shopping in Asunción](/shopping-in-asuncion/) guide breaks down both extremes, from the market
stalls to the malls.

## Day trips that fit easily into an Asunción stay

Asunción's real advantage is how much sits within reach of it. San Bernardino and Caacupé, on and
near Lake Ypacaraí, are under two hours away and make an easy day trip out of the city, one relaxed
and lakeside, the other centred on Paraguay's most important pilgrimage site. Cerro Corá National
Park, further north, combines hiking with a significant piece of 19th-century history. None of these
require an overnight if your schedule is tight, though San Bernardino and Caacupé both reward a
slower pace if you can spare it.

## Practical information

- **Best time to go:** April to October for cooler, more comfortable weather; December to February
  is very hot and humid, which changes how much walking you'll want to do.
- **Getting around:** taxis and rideshare apps are the easiest way to move between neighbourhoods;
  the historic centre and Costanera are walkable on their own, but Villa Morra and Mercado 4 are a
  drive from downtown.
- **What to bring:** comfortable walking shoes, sun protection, a reusable water bottle, and layers
  for evenings when the temperature drops after a hot day.
- **How long to stay:** two to three days covers the city itself comfortably; add a day or two for
  nearby San Bernardino, Caacupé or Cerro Corá if you have the time.
- **Safety:** Asunción is generally manageable for visitors who take normal city precautions. Check
  the [US State Department's Paraguay travel advisory](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/paraguay-travel-advisory.html)
  for current guidance, and see our [is Paraguay safe](/is-paraguay-safe/) guide for more detail.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need in Asunción?

Two to three days covers the main sights comfortably, including an evening in Villa Morra and a
morning at Mercado 4. Add extra time if you want to fold in a day trip to San Bernardino, Caacupé
or Cerro Corá without rushing back.

### Is Asunción walkable?

The historic centre and the Costanera are both walkable on their own, but the city as a whole is
spread out, and Villa Morra, Recoleta and Mercado 4 are better reached by taxi or rideshare,
especially in the heat.

### What's the best neighbourhood to stay in for a first visit?

Villa Morra or Recoleta suit most visitors well, being close to restaurants, cafés and shops, with
easy access to the historic centre by taxi. Downtown near the historic centre works too if you'd
rather walk to the main sights directly.

### Is Asunción worth visiting if I'm mainly here for Iguazú Falls or the Chaco?

Yes, even a couple of days is worthwhile. It's the natural hub for onward travel around Paraguay,
and it has enough of its own character, food and history to be more than a layover.

### What should I avoid doing in Asunción?

Avoid flashing valuables in crowded market areas like Mercado 4, and don't expect the same tourist
infrastructure you'd find in Buenos Aires or Rio: signage and English are both more limited, which
is part of what makes the city feel authentic rather than staged. If markets and malls are the
focus of your day, see our [guided shopping tour of Asunción](/shopping-asuncion/) for a route
through both.
