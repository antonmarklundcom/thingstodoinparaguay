Asunción doesn't have a single obvious "expat district" the way some Latin American capitals do — instead it has a handful of established residential areas, each with its own character, and the right one for you depends heavily on whether you're working remotely, raising kids, retiring, or just want to walk to good restaurants. Having spent time across most of them, here's an honest breakdown of the best neighborhoods to live in Asunción, without pretending any one of them is right for everyone.

## Villa Morra

Villa Morra is probably the closest thing Asunción has to a default answer for newcomers. It's central, walkable in parts, dense with cafés, restaurants and shopping (including the [Villa Morra food park](/villa-morra-food-park/)), and close to Shopping del Sol, one of the city's main malls. Apartment buildings here tend to be newer, with amenities like pools and gyms that are common in mid-to-high-end Paraguayan developments. The trade-off is that it's also one of the pricier areas to rent in, and traffic on the main avenues can be heavy at peak hours.

## Recoleta

Just north of Villa Morra, Recoleta is a leafier, somewhat quieter residential area popular with established expat families and diplomats — several embassies and international schools sit in or near this district. It has a more suburban, tree-lined feel than Villa Morra while still being a short drive from the same restaurants and shops. If you're relocating with kids and want proximity to international schooling options, this is usually near the top of the list.

## Carmelitas

Carmelitas sits between Villa Morra and the airport road, and has become popular with younger professionals and remote workers over the last several years — it has a good spread of newer apartment towers, coworking-friendly cafés, and decent access to the [remote work cafés and coworking spaces](/remote-work-life-in-asuncion-cafes-coworking-internet-you-can-rely-on/) that matter if you're working online. It tends to be slightly more affordable than the Villa Morra core while still feeling central.

## Las Lomas and Manorá

For those wanting more space, a house rather than an apartment, or a quieter setting without giving up proximity to the city, areas like Las Lomas offer larger residential plots and a more suburban pace. These areas suit families prioritising space and a garden over walkability to restaurants and nightlife.

## The historic centre (Centro)

Asunción's downtown core — colonial architecture, government buildings, the riverside — has genuine character and much lower rents, but it's a different lifestyle choice: more urban grit, fewer of the modern amenities newer developments offer, and less of a residential feel outside office hours. Some people love living close to the old city's history and river views; most relocating expats find the newer northern neighborhoods a more comfortable everyday fit.

## Practical information

- **Best areas for first-time relocators:** Villa Morra or Recoleta, for the combination of amenities, safety perception and rental supply
- **Best for remote workers on a budget:** Carmelitas, for newer apartments closer to coworking spots at a somewhat lower price point than Villa Morra's core
- **Best for families with school-age kids:** Recoleta, given its proximity to several international schools

## Choosing among the best neighborhoods to live in Asunción

Don't over-optimise this decision before you've spent time in the city. Many relocators rent a short-term place in Villa Morra or Recoleta for the first month, explore on foot and by rideshare, and then sign a longer lease once they understand which commute, noise level and grocery situation actually suits their routine. For general background on the city itself, the [Wikipedia entry on Asunción](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n) is a useful starting point on its history and layout.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the safest neighborhood to live in Asunción?

Villa Morra, Recoleta and Carmelitas are generally considered among the safer, more established residential areas, with visible private security common in apartment buildings. As anywhere, exercise normal city precautions and check current safety guidance — see our [is Paraguay safe](/is-paraguay-safe/) guide for a broader picture.

### Is it better to rent an apartment or a house in Asunción?

Most expats and remote workers start in an apartment for the security, amenities and simpler maintenance, especially in Villa Morra, Recoleta or Carmelitas. Houses suit families wanting more space and are more common in outer residential areas like Las Lomas.

### How much should I budget to rent in a good neighborhood?

Rental costs vary by neighborhood, apartment size and finish level, and shift over time, so check current listings directly rather than relying on a fixed figure here — our [apartment-hunting service](/apartment-hunting/) can also give you an up-to-date read on the market.

### Do I need to speak Spanish to live comfortably in these areas?

It helps a great deal for daily life — dealing with landlords, utilities and local services — though in Villa Morra, Recoleta and Carmelitas you'll find more English speakers than elsewhere in the city, particularly in newer cafés, coworking spaces and international-facing businesses.

### Which neighborhood is closest to the international schools?

Recoleta and the areas immediately around it have the highest concentration of international and bilingual schools, which is a major reason it's popular with relocating families — confirm current enrolment and locations directly with schools as they can change.
