Paraguay is an "off-the-beaten-path" destination, so it is normal to have doubts. Whether you are coming for a 3-day adventure or a lifetime move, we believe clarity is the first step to a great trip.

Is Paraguay safe for tourists?
The Honest Answer: Compared to its neighbors (Brazil/Argentina), Paraguay is statistically safer regarding violent crime. However, petty crime exists in major cities. Our Advice: We advise clients to avoid walking alone at night in downtown Asunción or Ciudad del Este. When you book our private drivers or tours, you are in a "bubble" of safety. Do I need to speak Spanish?
In Asunción: At high-end hotels and restaurants, you will find some English speakers. In the Interior: English is almost non-existent. Guaraní and Spanish are the norm. How We Help: This is why our service is essential. Anton, Yanina and our team speak fluent English. We act as your translators and fixers so you never feel lost or misunderstood.

When is the best time to visit?
The Sweet Spot: March to May (Autumn) and September to November (Spring). The temperatures are pleasant (20°C - 30°C). The Extreme: Summer (Dec-Feb) is very hot (often 40°C+). If you visit then, we schedule tours early in the morning and prioritize air-conditioned vehicles and pools.

Cash or Card? How do payments work?
The Mix: Major credit cards are accepted in malls, supermarkets, and nice restaurants in Asunción. The Reality: For taxis, street food, small crafts, or rural towns, you need cash (Guaranies - PYG). Tip: We recommend bringing USD (clean, new bills) to exchange here. ATMs are widely available but charge high withdrawal fees for foreign cards.

Can I drink the tap water?
In Asunción: The tap water is treated and technically potable, but the mineral content is different and can upset sensitive stomachs. Our Recommendation: We strongly advise all our international clients to stick to bottled water to ensure your trip isn't ruined by a stomach bug.

# Recurring Template Pattern (for componentizing the HTML/PHP rebuild)

The 18 real "Tour/Service" pages (Paraguay Culture Tour, Jesuit Ruins Tour, Salto Cristal Tour, Itaipu Dam Tour, Yerba Mate Tour, San Bernardino Trip, Bird Watching, Fishing Charters, Iguazu Falls from Asuncion, Paraguay Tour, Apartment Hunting, School Placement, Paraguay Residency Service, Private Driver, Paraguay Real Estate Tour, Airport Transfer, Healthcare Paraguay, Travel Planner) all share ONE Elementor template with this section order — this should become a single reusable page template in the rebuild:

1. **Hero** — H1 = service/tour name, H3 tagline, one short intro paragraph (meta-description-style), primary CTA button (e.g. "BOOK MY X" / "START MY ADVENTURE").
2. **Problem/Hook section** — H3 emotional headline + 2 paragraphs framing the traveler's pain point (e.g. "hard to reach," "confusing logistics," "language barrier").
3. **Solution section** — H3 "how we solve it" headline + 1–2 paragraphs on the service's approach.
4. **Itinerary/Breakdown section** ("The Journey" / "The Treasures" / "The Process" / "What's Inside") — H3 section label, then 3–4 H2 sub-stops/sub-items, each with its own descriptive paragraph. This is the core content block, unique per page.
5. **"Why Choose This Tour?" section** — 3 short H2/H3 sub-benefits, each with one paragraph.
6. **"Practical Information" section** — bullet-style key facts: Duration, Departure time, Transport type, What to Bring, Requirements, Best Season, etc. (label set varies slightly per page but the pattern is consistent).
7. **FAQ section** — H2 "FAQ" + standard intro paragraph ("We believe a great experience starts with clear communication...") + 7–9 accordion Q&As. Only the first Q&A per page was captured expanded; the rest are collapsed accordions — a full DB/CMS export is recommended over scraping to capture every collapsed FAQ answer for migration.
8. **Closing CTA banner** — H2 emotional closer + 1–2 paragraphs + final CTA button (routes to /contact/ in every case — no WhatsApp links, no visible pricing, no live booking widget anywhere on the current site).
9. Shared sitewide footer.

Standalone rich pages (Home, /, About2, Day Trips, FAQ2, Restaurants Guide, Bars Tour, Shopping, Souvenirs) use a looser variant of the same visual language (hero + sections + FAQ + CTA) but aren't as rigidly identical — treat as one-off page builds referencing the same design system (Elementor/Hello Elementor visual style: card grids for destinations/tours, accordion FAQs, testimonial quote blocks with named staff — Anton Marklund & Yanina Alvarez appear repeatedly as the real people behind the brand).

The 33 blog posts use a second, much simpler template: H1 title, byline (author "Yanina", date), category badge, single H3 "Giving You The Best Services Experiences" body heading, body paragraphs, tags line, share row, WordPress default comment form. Since all body content must be rewritten from scratch, this is a good opportunity to redesign this template entirely for the rebuild (e.g. as a proper blog-post layout with real hero image, TOC, related-posts, etc.) rather than preserving the current thin template.
