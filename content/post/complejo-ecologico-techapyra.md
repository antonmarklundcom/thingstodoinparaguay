Complejo Ecológico Techapyrã is one of those places Paraguayans mention with a grin before they explain it: a forest park in Caaguazú department where walking trails wind past life-size dinosaur replicas standing among the native trees. It sounds like a novelty stop, and it is, but it is also a genuinely pleasant patch of woodland to slow down in in a country where so much of the interior gets driven past rather than visited.

## What Complejo Ecológico Techapyrã actually is

The park sits on private land that has been given over to a mix of conservation and family recreation, which is a common model for smaller eco-parks in Paraguay's interior — a landowner protects a stand of native forest and opens it to visitors instead of clearing it. At Techapyrã that forest is dotted with dinosaur sculptures along the walking paths, which is the detail that gets it shared on social media, but the actual draw for most visitors ends up being simpler: shaded trails, birdsong instead of traffic noise, and enough open grass to let kids run without anyone worrying about the road.

Caaguazú department is part of [Paraguay's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay) agricultural heartland, mostly soy and cattle country these days, so a pocket of standing native forest here reads differently than it would in, say, the Atlantic Forest reserves further east. It's a reminder of what the region looked like before the land was cleared for farming, packaged as a day out rather than a formal reserve.

## Getting there

Techapyrã sits roughly on the corridor most travelers already use to get from Asunción to Ciudad del Este, which makes it a natural halfway stop rather than a special detour. If you're driving yourself, plan for unpaved side roads near the entrance even though the main highway is paved the whole way — Paraguayan interior roads often are. There's no public transport that drops you at the gate, so this is a trip for a rental car, a private driver, or a tour that includes it as a stop.

If you don't want to deal with logistics, a private driver or a day-trip tour that bundles Techapyrã with other Caaguazú-area stops is the easiest route, especially if you're also planning to continue on to Ciudad del Este or Itaipú the same trip.

## What to expect on the trails

Bring good shoes. The trails are mostly packed earth and can turn muddy after rain, and Caaguazú's climate is humid enough that even a dry-looking morning can leave the undergrowth wet. The dinosaur replicas are scattered at intervals along the main loop, sized and posed to look genuinely striking against the tree line rather than crammed together like a theme park, and there are usually picnic tables and open grass areas near the entrance for families who want to make a half-day of it rather than just walk through.

Because it's a working eco-park rather than a national park with a ranger station, facilities are simple. Don't expect a visitor center with exhibits — the appeal here is the walk itself, the trees, and the slightly surreal juxtaposition of prehistoric sculpture in a very real subtropical forest. If you're the kind of traveler who likes [Paraguay's wilder national parks](/paraguay-national-parks/) but is short on time, Techapyrã is a gentler, more accessible taste of the same idea.

## Practical information

- **Getting there:** by car or private driver on the Asunción–Ciudad del Este corridor, in Caaguazú department; no direct public transport to the entrance
- **Best time to visit:** the drier winter months (roughly June to August) keep the trails less muddy; go early to beat the midday heat in summer
- **What to bring:** closed walking shoes, insect repellent, sun protection, drinking water and cash, since rural stops like this are unlikely to take cards
- **Time needed:** a couple of hours for the main trail loop, longer if you picnic
- **Good for:** families with kids, road-trippers breaking up the drive to Ciudad del Este, anyone who likes a walk in the woods with a bit of whimsy
- **Check ahead:** confirm current opening hours and entry cost before you go, since these can change without much online notice for smaller rural attractions

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Complejo Ecológico Techapyrã worth a detour?
If you're already driving the Asunción–Ciudad del Este route, yes — it breaks up a long highway drive with a shaded walk and something genuinely different to look at. If you'd have to go significantly out of your way just for it, weigh it against other stops in [Paraguay's national parks](/paraguay-national-parks/) that offer more untouched forest.

### Is it suitable for young children?
Generally yes. The trails are flat enough for most kids to manage, and the dinosaur theme tends to be a hit with younger visitors. Keep an eye on footing after rain, and bring water and shade hats since there's limited shelter on parts of the loop.

### Do I need a guide?
No — the trails are walkable on your own. A guide isn't necessary, though joining a day tour that includes Techapyrã as one stop among several can be a convenient way to see it without arranging your own transport.

### How does it compare to Paraguay's national parks?
It's smaller and more curated — a private eco-park with a theme, not a protected wilderness area. If you want the real thing, places covered in our guide to [Paraguay's national parks](/paraguay-national-parks/) or the [Atlantic Forest](/atlantic-forest-paraguay/) are the better comparison. Techapyrã is closer to a family day out than a nature expedition.

### What should I bring that people usually forget?
Cash, insect repellent and a hat. Rural Caaguazú isn't set up for card payments the way Asunción is, and the humidity brings out mosquitoes even on cooler days.
