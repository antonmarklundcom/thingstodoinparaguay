Paraguay beaches are not the first thing that comes to mind when people plan a trip to a landlocked
country, and that reputation undersells the place. Where the Paraná, Paraguay and Ypané rivers widen and slow down, they leave behind long
stretches of pale sand that look, from a photo, like a stretch of ocean coast. Locals have been
building their summers around these river beaches for generations, and if you visit between
December and February you will find them just as central to Paraguayan life as an asado on a
Sunday. This guide covers the best Paraguay beaches, how to reach them, and what an afternoon
there actually looks like.

## The best Paraguay beaches and why they work

The Paraná River forms much of Paraguay's eastern and southern border, and in several places —
especially around Encarnación and further south near the Argentine border — the water table drops
in the dry months and exposes broad sandbanks. Add a few decades of small tourism infrastructure
(showers, snack stalls, lifeguard posts in the busiest spots) and you get a genuine beach scene,
just with fresh water instead of salt. The water is warm through the summer, the currents in the
developed swimming areas are gentle, and because these are river beaches rather than ocean coast,
there's no tide to plan around and no salt residue to rinse off afterward.

The single most popular destination is **Villa Florida**, a small town on the Tebicuary River
about two and a half hours from Asunción by car or bus. Paraguayans call it the "beach capital"
of the interior, and in January the riverside strip fills with families, sound systems, and stalls
selling chipa and cold terere. The sand here is genuinely fine and white, the far bank is wooded
and undeveloped, and the whole town essentially exists to serve the beach in summer and goes quiet
the rest of the year.

## Encarnación and the Costanera

Further south, **Encarnación** built a long municipal Costanera along the Paraná after the Yacyretá
dam raised the river level and reshaped the shoreline. The Costanera itself is more promenade than
wild beach — paved paths, green space, a series of artificial beach coves with imported sand — but
it's a pleasant, walkable stretch with the Argentine city of Posadas visible across the water.
Nearby, more rustic river beaches along the Paraná toward the Itapúa countryside give a quieter,
less manicured alternative if the Costanera feels too built up for your taste.

## Smaller river beaches worth seeking out

Beyond the two big names, a scattering of smaller riverside spots serve local communities and
reward anyone willing to drive off the main routes:

- Stretches of the **Ypané and Aquidabán rivers** in the north, near San Pedro and Concepción
  departments, popular with locals but rarely visited by foreign tourists.
- Riverside beaches near **Ayolas**, close to the Yacyretá dam, worth combining with a look at the
  dam itself if you're already in the area.
- Smaller sandbanks along the Paraguay River near the capital, though these see heavier boat
  traffic and are best treated as a scenic stop rather than a swimming destination.

If you'd rather not chase down river beaches on your own, a local operator can build a day trip
around Villa Florida or Encarnación with transport sorted — worth considering if you're short on
time or don't want to navigate rural bus routes.

## Practical information

- **Best time to go:** December through February (Paraguayan summer), when river levels are lower
  and the sand exposed. Water levels and beach conditions change year to year, so check locally
  before a long trip out.
- **Getting there:** Villa Florida sits on Route 1 between Asunción and Encarnación — reachable by
  long-distance bus from Asunción's Terminal de Ómnibus or by car in a little over two hours.
  Encarnación has its own bus terminal with frequent connections from the capital.
  See our [guide to the Asunción bus terminal](/asuncion-bus-terminal/) before you travel.
- **What to bring:** Strong sunscreen, a hat, insect repellent for the evening, and cash — many
  riverside stalls and smaller towns don't take cards.
- **Water safety:** Stick to the areas with lifeguards or a visibly established swimming zone,
  especially with kids, since currents can be stronger away from the marked shallows.
- **Combine it with:** a stop in [Caacupé](/caacupe/) or a wider loop through the interior if you're
  driving rather than flying between stops.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Paraguay actually have beaches if it's landlocked?

Yes — they're river beaches rather than ocean coast, formed where the Paraná, Tebicuary and other
rivers widen and expose sandbanks in the dry season. Villa Florida and Encarnación's Costanera are
the two best known.

### Is the water clean enough to swim in?

In the established swimming areas, yes, and it's a normal part of Paraguayan summer life. As with
any river, conditions can vary after heavy rain upstream, so ask locally if the water looks
unusually high or discoloured before swimming.

### When is the best time to visit Paraguay's beaches?

December to February is peak season, when the weather is hot and river levels tend to be lower,
exposing more sand. Outside those months the beaches are far quieter and some seasonal stalls
close.

### Can I visit Villa Florida as a day trip from Asunción?

Yes, it's a round trip of around five hours of driving, so it's doable in a day if you leave early,
though most visitors prefer to stay a night to enjoy the evening atmosphere on the riverside.

### Are there beaches close to Asunción itself?

Nothing comparable to Villa Florida directly in the capital, though smaller riverside spots exist
along the Paraguay River nearby. For a proper beach day, Villa Florida or Encarnación are worth the
drive.
