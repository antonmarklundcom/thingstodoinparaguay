faq:
  - q: Can foreigners buy property in Paraguay?
    a: Yes. Foreigners have the same property rights as citizens. You do not need to be a resident to buy land or an apartment (with the exception of land strictly on the border lines with Brazil/Argentina, which has restrictions).
  - q: Do I need a bank account in Paraguay to buy?
    a: Not necessarily to make an offer, but you'll usually want one before closing. Most sellers and notaries expect payment handled in a traceable way, and a local account makes wiring funds and paying taxes far simpler. We can introduce you to notaries who work regularly with foreign buyers on this.
  - q: What are the closing costs?
    a: Expect notary fees, registration fees, and a transfer tax on top of the purchase price. These vary by property type and transaction size, so check current rates with your notary once you have a specific property in mind.
  - q: Is financing available for foreigners?
    a: Local bank financing for non-residents is limited and the terms aren't especially generous, so most international buyers we work with pay cash or arrange financing from their home country. Ask us and we can connect you with an advisor who can walk through the current options.
  - q: How do I know the title is clean?
    a: This is the most critical step. Never hand over money without a "Certificado de Condiciones de Dominio" checked by a trusted Notary. We connect you with reputable professionals to do this due diligence.
  - q: Can we customize the leather goods?
    a: This scouting tour focuses on land and property, so there's no artisan workshop stop built into the day. If you'd like to combine a market visit with your trip, ask us to add one, or see our [souvenir shopping tour](/paraguay-souvenirs/) for a dedicated artisan itinerary.
  - q: Can I get residency by buying property?
    a: Buying property alone doesn't grant residency — that's a separate process with its own requirements. If residency is part of your plan, our [residency service](/paraguay-residency-service/) can run alongside your property search.
  - q: Are squatters a problem?
    a: Land invasions (ocupaciones) do happen, particularly on rural land that sits vacant or has an unclear title. It's one more reason title verification matters, and why we flag any property without an active caretaker or recent use. We can point you toward land with a track record of being actively managed.
