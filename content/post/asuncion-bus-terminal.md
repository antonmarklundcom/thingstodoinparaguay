The Asunción bus terminal, officially the Terminal de Ómnibus de Asunción and known locally simply as "la Terminal", is the hub almost every overland trip in Paraguay eventually passes through. It sits on Avenida Fernando de la Mora in the Sajonia area, a short taxi or rideshare ride from the city centre, and it's where dozens of bus companies run everything from local city routes to long-distance coaches to Encarnación, Ciudad del Este, the Chaco, and international services to Buenos Aires and São Paulo. For a country without passenger rail, the bus terminal is genuinely the backbone of getting around.

## What to expect inside the Asunción bus terminal

The terminal is a large, functional building organised around ticket counters (boleterías) for the different bus companies, each with its own window and its own schedules and prices posted. It can feel a little overwhelming on a first visit, since there's no single unified timetable board the way some European or North American stations have it, so you generally walk the row of counters and compare. Company names to look out for include the larger long-distance operators that run the Asunción–Ciudad del Este and Asunción–Encarnación corridors frequently throughout the day, as well as smaller regional companies serving specific towns.

Platforms (plataformas) are numbered and signed, and departures are usually announced, but it pays to double-check your platform with terminal staff or the company's counter shortly before departure since gate assignments can shift. If you're travelling with luggage, most long-distance coaches have an under-bus hold; keep your ticket stub, as it's often what you show to reclaim your bag at the other end.

## Getting to and from the terminal

The terminal is well connected to Asunción's city bus network, and a taxi or rideshare from the centre or from Villa Morra typically takes 15 to 25 minutes depending on traffic. If you're flying in, a transfer straight from [Silvio Pettirossi airport](/airport-transfer-paraguay/) to the terminal is a sensible way to connect onward without doubling back through the city, especially if your bus leaves the same day you land.

Inside and around the terminal there are small shops, food stalls, and pharmacies, useful for grabbing supplies before a long overnight ride. As with any busy transport hub anywhere in the world, keep bags close and valuables out of easy reach, particularly during the busiest departure windows.

## Practical information

- **Location:** Avenida Fernando de la Mora, Sajonia, Asunción, roughly 15 to 25 minutes by taxi from the city centre
- **What it serves:** city and metro-area buses, long-distance domestic coaches (Encarnación, Ciudad del Este, the Chaco, Concepción and beyond), and international routes to Argentina and Brazil
- **Buying tickets:** at the individual company's counter inside the terminal; check current fares and schedules directly with the operator, as these change seasonally
- **What to bring:** small cash in guaraníes for tickets and snacks, your passport or ID for long-distance and international routes, and a jacket or light blanket, since overnight coaches run strong air conditioning
- **Best time to arrive:** at least 30–45 minutes before a long-distance departure to buy your ticket and find the right platform without rushing
- **Luggage:** keep your baggage claim stub from the under-bus hold; a small daypack with essentials is worth keeping with you rather than checking

## Planning a longer trip

If your onward journey is toward the [Jesuit missions](/jesuit-missions-paraguay/) in the south or the falls at [Iguazu](/iguazu-falls/) near Ciudad del Este, the long-distance coaches from this terminal are the standard and most economical way to get there, and Paraguay's coach network is more extensive than many first-time visitors expect. For background on Paraguay's geography and how its cities connect, the [Wikipedia entry on Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay) is a useful primer before you plan a multi-stop route.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Asunción bus terminal safe?

It's a large, busy public transport hub and generally fine to use, but like any major terminal it draws pickpockets during peak hours. Keep valuables in front pockets or a zipped bag, stay aware in crowded queues, and avoid displaying cash or phones unnecessarily.

### Do I need to book long-distance bus tickets in advance?

For most routes you can buy your ticket at the counter on the day, and companies run frequent departures on the busiest corridors like Asunción–Ciudad del Este. During peak holiday periods such as Christmas, New Year or Semana Santa, buying a day or two ahead is safer since seats can sell out.

### How do I know which company to choose?

Compare the posted schedules and prices at each counter. Most companies serving the same route offer broadly similar service, though some run newer coaches with more amenities (air conditioning, reclining seats, onboard toilets) on longer overnight hauls. Ask at the counter what class of service you're buying.

### Can I get an international bus from here to Argentina or Brazil?

Yes, several companies run direct international coaches from the Asunción terminal to Buenos Aires and to Brazilian cities, with a border-crossing stop for immigration. Bring your passport and check current visa and entry requirements before travelling.

### Is there luggage storage at the terminal if I have a long layover?

Some counters and terminal services offer short-term luggage storage for a fee, though availability varies by operator. Ask at the information desk when you arrive if you need to store bags for a few hours between connections.
