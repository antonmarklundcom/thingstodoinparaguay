Shopping in Asunción swings between two very different worlds, and the fun is in visiting both.
On one side there are the covered stalls of Mercado 4, loud, cheap and slightly chaotic, where
vendors sell everything from ao po'i embroidery to phone cases. On the other are air-conditioned
malls like Shopping del Sol and Paseo La Galería, which feel like shopping centres anywhere in
South America, complete with food courts and cinemas. A day spent moving between the two gives
you a much better sense of the city than either extreme on its own.

## Mercado 4 and the old-town markets

Mercado 4 is the biggest and most-talked-about market in Asunción, a sprawling grid of covered
aisles just west of the microcentro. It is not a tourist market first, it is where a large part
of the city actually buys its groceries, clothes, tools and electronics, which is exactly why it
is worth a visit. Go in the morning when it is busiest but still manageable, keep valuables in a
front pocket or zipped bag, and expect narrow aisles and a lot of noise. Look for stalls selling
yerba mate and the gourds and bombillas (metal straws) used to drink it, since a mate set makes a
far more authentic souvenir than anything shrink-wrapped in a mall gift shop.

Closer to the historic centre, smaller shops around Palma and Estrella streets sell leather goods,
including belts, wallets and boots, often at a fraction of what you would pay for similar quality
elsewhere. Paraguay has a real leather-working tradition and prices reflect local labour costs, not
import markups, so this is one category where it genuinely pays to shop locally rather than at the
airport on your way out. For background on the city these markets sit in, see the
[Asunción Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asunci%C3%B3n).

## The malls: Shopping del Sol, Paseo La Galería and Shopping Mariscal

If Mercado 4 is the sensory-overload option, Shopping del Sol in the Las Mercedes/Aviadores del
Chaco area is the reset button. It is the largest and most established mall in the city, with
international and Paraguayan clothing chains, a large supermarket, a food court and a cinema. It
is a genuinely useful stop on a hot afternoon, both for shopping and simply for air conditioning
and a coffee.

Paseo La Galería, in the same general part of town, leans more upscale, with a design skewed
toward younger shoppers and a cluster of casual restaurants around its open-air sections. Shopping
Mariscal, nearer the centre, is smaller and more local in feel, useful if you are staying downtown
and do not want to cross the city for a mall run. None of these will surprise anyone who has
shopped in Buenos Aires or São Paulo, but they are reliable, safe and a good gauge of everyday
middle-class Asunción life, which is part of what makes them worth a look even if you do not buy
anything.

## Ao po'i, ñandutí lace and where to find the real thing

Two textiles are worth learning to recognise before you shop for souvenirs. Ao po'i is a fine,
hand-embroidered cotton fabric traditionally used for shirts, blouses and tablecloths, mostly
associated with the town of Yataity. Ñandutí is Paraguay's famous spiderweb lace, made by hand in
concentric, radiating patterns, historically centred on the town of Itauguá just outside Asunción.
Both show up in craft stores and at the Mercado de Artesanía stalls in the capital, but quality and
authenticity vary a great deal. Handmade pieces take real time to produce and are priced
accordingly; anything suspiciously cheap and uniform is more likely machine-made. If you want the
genuine article and have the time, a day trip out to Itauguá to buy directly from lacemakers is a
better bet than a rushed purchase in the city.

## Practical tips for shopping in Asunción

- **Best time to go:** weekday mornings for markets, when stalls are fully stocked and aisles are
  less crowded; malls are comfortable any time but busiest on weekend evenings.
- **Getting there:** Shopping del Sol and Paseo La Galería sit close together off Avenida Aviadores
  del Chaco, an easy taxi or rideshare from most hotels; Mercado 4 is nearer downtown and walkable
  from the historic centre if you don't mind heat and traffic.
- **What to bring:** small bills and coins for market stalls, a bag or backpack you can keep in
  front of you, and comfortable shoes, since Mercado 4's aisles are uneven underfoot.
- **Bargaining:** modest haggling is normal and expected at market stalls, much less so in malls or
  fixed-price craft shops.
- **Payment:** carry cash for markets. Cards are widely accepted in malls, less reliably at street
  stalls, so check current terms before assuming a card will work.
- **Language:** Spanish and Guaraní are both spoken; a few basic Spanish phrases go a long way with
  market vendors who don't deal with tourists daily.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Mercado 4 safe for tourists?

It is generally fine during the day if you take normal precautions: keep bags zipped and in front
of you, avoid flashing phones or cash, and stay aware of your surroundings in crowded aisles. Going
with a local guide or a small group is a reasonable option if you would rather not navigate it
solo the first time.

### What souvenirs are actually made in Paraguay?

Ñandutí lace, ao po'i embroidered cotton, leather goods, wood carvings and yerba mate gourds
(guampas) with their bombilla straws are all genuinely Paraguayan crafts. Look for stalls or shops
that can tell you where a piece was made; a specific town name is usually a good sign.

### Are prices fixed in Asunción's malls?

Yes. Malls like Shopping del Sol operate on fixed retail pricing the same as malls anywhere, so
haggling is not expected or usually welcomed. Bargaining is a market-stall and street-vendor thing,
not a mall thing.

### Can I use a credit card at Mercado 4?

Some larger stalls accept cards, but many small vendors are cash-only. Bring guaraníes in small
denominations to avoid problems making change, and check current acceptance before relying on a
card for a specific purchase.

### How much time should I set aside for shopping in Asunción?

A half day covers a decent visit to Mercado 4 plus one mall. A full day lets you add a craft-focused
stop for ñandutí or ao po'i, or a side trip to Itauguá, without feeling rushed.
