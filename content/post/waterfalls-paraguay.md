Paraguay lives in the shadow of its neighbours when it comes to waterfalls, mostly because Iguazú
Falls sits just across the border and understandably dominates the conversation. But the
waterfalls Paraguay keeps for itself are worth seeking out too, from a hidden crystal-clear cascade
in the Chaco's dry forest to a
smaller, quieter set of falls near Ciudad del Este that most visitors to the region never bother
to see. None of them compete with Iguazú in scale, and that's fine: they offer something Iguazú
can't, which is a good hike, a swim, and often nobody else around.

## Salto Cristal, in the Chaco

Salto Cristal is Paraguay's best-known waterfall away from the Iguazú area, tucked into the dry
forest of the Gran Chaco near Filadelfia. Reaching it typically means a guided hike through scrub
forest that opens onto a small, clear waterfall dropping into a natural pool, cool enough to be a
genuine relief after a hot walk in. It is remote by Paraguayan standards, which is part of the
appeal: the Chaco sees a fraction of the visitors the rest of the country does, so a morning at
Salto Cristal often feels closer to a private discovery than a tourist stop. It pairs naturally
with a broader look at the region on our [Filadelfia and the Chaco](/filadelfia-chaco/) guide, and
our [Salto Cristal tour](/salto-cristal-tour/) handles the logistics of getting out there with a
guide who knows the trail.

## The Monday Falls, near Ciudad del Este

Closer to the eastern border, the Monday Falls (Saltos del Monday) sit within a short drive of
Ciudad del Este and, by extension, of the much larger Iguazú Falls just beyond. They are far
smaller than their famous neighbour, but that scale makes them easy to see properly in an hour or
two, with viewing platforms and walking trails through surrounding forest. For visitors already
in the area for Iguazú or the Itaipu Dam, the Monday Falls are a natural add-on rather than a
separate trip, best folded into a longer day exploring [Ciudad del Este](/exploring-ciudad-del-este/)
and the region around it. Compare them, if you like, to their much larger neighbour on our
[Iguazú Falls](/iguazu-falls/) guide, and see the
[Iguazu Falls Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iguazu_Falls) for the wider geography
of the area, since the falls straddle the Argentina–Brazil border a short distance from Paraguay.

## Other waterfalls Paraguay hides further afield

Beyond the two headline falls, Paraguay's national parks and forest reserves hide a scattering of
smaller cascades and natural pools, particularly in the wetter, more forested east of the country
near the Atlantic Forest remnants. These rarely have formal infrastructure beyond a marked
trailhead at best, and are best visited with a local guide who knows current access and trail conditions,
since routes and water levels can change with the season. Our
[Paraguay national parks](/paraguay-national-parks/) and
[Atlantic Forest](/atlantic-forest-paraguay/) guides are good starting points if you want to build a
longer nature-focused itinerary around these smaller sites rather than just the two main falls.

## Practical information

- **Best time to go:** the dry season, roughly April to October, gives the most reliable trail
  conditions; water volume at smaller falls can drop noticeably late in the dry season, so if a
  full flow matters to you, aim for its earlier months or just after rain.
- **Getting there:** Salto Cristal requires a guided trip from Filadelfia or an organised tour from
  Asunción, given the distance and the unmarked nature of Chaco trails; the Monday Falls are an
  easy add-on from Ciudad del Este with regular local transport or a short taxi ride.
- **What to bring:** sturdy walking shoes, a swimsuit if you plan to get in the water, sun
  protection, insect repellent, and more water than you think you'll need, especially for the Chaco
  heat.
- **Fitness level:** both main falls involve some walking on uneven, sometimes muddy ground; neither
  requires technical hiking experience, but flat shoes are not the right choice.
- **Guides:** a local guide is strongly recommended for Salto Cristal given the remoteness and lack
  of signage, and genuinely useful at the Monday Falls for context on the surrounding forest.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Paraguay's waterfalls as impressive as Iguazú Falls?

No, and they don't try to be. Iguazú is one of the largest waterfall systems in the world; Paraguay's
own falls are smaller and quieter, valued more for the hike and the setting than for sheer scale.

### Can I visit Salto Cristal without a guide?

It's not recommended. The Chaco is remote, trails are not well marked, and conditions can change
with the season. A local guide makes the trip safer and considerably easier to plan.

### Is it possible to swim at these waterfalls?

Yes, at both Salto Cristal and several of the smaller cascades further east, water conditions
permitting. Check current conditions with your guide before swimming, particularly after heavy
rain when currents can be stronger.

### How far is the Monday Falls from Ciudad del Este?

It's a short drive, easily combined with a visit to the Itaipu Dam or a border crossing toward
Iguazú Falls on the same day. Check current transport options if you're not travelling with a
guide.

### What should I wear to visit a waterfall in Paraguay?

Comfortable closed shoes for the walk in, a swimsuit under your clothes if you plan to get in the
water, and sun protection. The Chaco in particular can be very hot even in the cooler months, so
dress for heat regardless of the season.
