faq:
  - q: Do I need a Visa for this tour?
    a: No. We visit the Paraguayan side of the dam. As long as you have your valid passport (which is required for entry to the facility), no visa is needed because we do not legally exit Paraguay.
  - q: Can we cross to Brazil?
    a: Not as part of this tour — we visit the Paraguayan side of the dam only, so there's no border crossing or extra paperwork. If you want to add Brazil or [Ciudad del Este](/exploring-ciudad-del-este/) to your trip, ask us and we'll help you plan the logistics separately.
  - q: Is the "Technical Tour" (Inside) better than the "Panoramic" (Outside)?
    a: They show different things. The Panoramic tour covers the exterior, the spillway, and the top of the dam wall by bus. The Technical Tour goes inside to the turbine hall, which is more dramatic but requires closed shoes and follows a stricter schedule. Ask us which fits your day when you book.
  - q: Will we see the water flowing (The Spillway)?
    a: It depends on the reservoir level and how much water the operators are releasing that day — check current conditions with us closer to your visit. Even when the sluice gates are closed, the scale of the spillway itself is worth seeing.
  - q: Can we shop in Ciudad del Este on this trip?
    a: Ciudad del Este is a massive shopping hub. If you want to shop, we can add a stop, but please note that traffic there is very heavy and it will extend the day significantly. We recommend a dedicated day for shopping if that is your priority.
  - q: Is this tour safe for kids?
    a: Yes, generally — the panoramic route is a comfortable bus ride, but the Technical Tour involves stairs and machinery noise, so let us know the ages of any kids in your group so we can suggest the right version.
  - q: Is lunch included?
    a: No, lunch isn't included, but we can point you to good options near the Visitor Center or in Ciudad del Este and build a stop into the day.
  - q: What should I wear?
    a: Closed shoes are required for the Technical Tour (no flip-flops), and light, breathable clothing is best either way since there's little shade around the facility.
