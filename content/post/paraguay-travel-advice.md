Good Paraguay travel advice is hard to find online, mostly because so few English-language guides
cover the country in any depth compared to its neighbors. That gap is exactly what this piece is
for — the practical basics that make the difference between a smooth trip and a frustrating one,
covering documents, money, transport and everyday safety, written for anyone visiting for the
first time or planning a longer relocation trip.

## Paraguay travel advice: entry requirements and documents

Requirements vary by nationality and change periodically, so the single most useful thing you can
do before booking flights is check the current entry rules for your passport directly with a
Paraguayan consulate or your home country's foreign ministry — do not rely on a blog post, this one
included, for the current visa situation. Keep a printed and digital copy of your passport, return
flight information and any accommodation confirmation, since border officials in South America
commonly ask to see onward travel proof. If you're arriving by land from Argentina or Brazil,
budget extra time at border crossings, particularly around Ciudad del Este and Encarnación, where
queues can be unpredictable.

## Money and cards

Paraguay's currency is the guaraní (PYG), and while cards are increasingly accepted in Asunción's
malls, hotels and larger restaurants, cash is still king almost everywhere else — smaller towns,
markets, local buses and roadside stalls. Withdraw guaraníes from ATMs in bank branches or shopping
centers rather than isolated street machines, and carry small bills, since vendors outside the
capital frequently can't break large notes. US dollars are useful as a backup for larger purchases
or emergencies but aren't a substitute for having local currency day to day.

## Getting around

Long-distance buses connect every major city and are the backbone of budget travel here — see our
guide to the [Asunción bus terminal](/asuncion-bus-terminal/) for how the system works and what to
expect. Within Asunción, ride-hailing apps are a more predictable option than hailing a taxi on the
street. If you're planning to cover a lot of ground independently, especially toward the Chaco,
renting a car with a reliable 4x4 for unpaved stretches is worth the extra cost. For travelers who'd
rather not navigate logistics themselves, a [private driver](/private-driver/) or a

## Health and safety basics

Tap water in Asunción is treated, but many visitors still stick to bottled or filtered water
outside the capital as a precaution. Sun protection matters more than people expect — Paraguay's
subtropical climate means strong UV exposure even on cloudy days. Mosquito-borne illness is a real
consideration in the wetter months, so repellent is worth packing regardless of season. For safety
specifically, ordinary city precautions apply: avoid displaying valuables, use registered taxis or
ride-hailing apps at night, and keep an eye on your belongings in bus terminals and markets, which
are the most common spots for opportunistic theft. Our [Paraguay safety guide](/is-paraguay-safe/)
goes deeper on this if you want more detail before you travel.

## Practical information

- **Best time to visit:** April to September, the dry, cooler season, is the most comfortable for
  most travel; December through February is hot and humid but coincides with beach season on the
  rivers.
- **Language:** Spanish and Guaraní are both official languages; Spanish gets you through almost
  any tourist interaction, though a few words of Guaraní go a long way with locals.
- **What to bring:** a physical copy of key documents, a universal power adapter, sun protection,
  insect repellent, and cash in small denominations.
- **Connectivity:** a local SIM card is inexpensive and widely available at the airport or in
  Asunción's shopping centers if you want data on arrival.
- **Health:** check current vaccination and health guidance for the region before you travel — the
  [WHO's travel health advice](https://www.who.int/travel-advice) is a reliable starting point.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a visa to visit Paraguay?

It depends on your nationality and changes over time, so check directly with a Paraguayan consulate
or your government's travel advisory before booking, rather than relying on general guides.

### Is Paraguay safe for tourists?

Most visits are trouble-free with ordinary precautions. Petty theft in crowded areas like bus
terminals is the most common risk rather than anything more serious. See our full
[Paraguay safety guide](/is-paraguay-safe/) for more detail, and check your government's official
[travel advisory](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/traveladvisories/traveladvisories/paraguay-travel-advisory.html)
before you go.

### What currency should I bring?

Bring US dollars to exchange or withdraw guaraníes from ATMs on arrival. Cards work in the capital's
larger establishments but cash is essential once you leave Asunción.

### Is it easy to get around without speaking Spanish?

It's manageable in Asunción's tourist-facing businesses, but basic Spanish makes independent travel
in smaller towns and on buses considerably easier, since English is not widely spoken outside
hospitality settings.

### What should I pack that people forget?

Reef-safe or strong sunscreen, a reusable water bottle, mosquito repellent, and a portable phone
charger — power outlets and Wi-Fi reliability vary more outside the capital than most first-time
visitors expect.
