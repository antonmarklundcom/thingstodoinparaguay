  ## You Know the Drink. Now Meet the Legend.

  This yerba mate tour takes you to the source of "Green Gold" — the plantations and drying houses of southern Paraguay where the world's mate actually comes from. Yerba mate is spreading everywhere, from the locker rooms of professional athletes to the shelves of health food stores abroad, but drinking it from a can isn't the same as understanding where it comes from.

  The real story is here, in the red soil of Paraguay, where the plant was first cultivated by the Guaraní and later domesticated at scale by the Jesuits. Navigating the rural "Ka'a Rape" (Yerba Mate Route) on your own is hard — the best plantations sit deep in the German colonies of the south, around Bella Vista, and factory tours usually require an appointment arranged in advance.

faq:
  - q: Is it true that Yerba Mate has more caffeine than coffee?
    a: It's similar, but the effect is different. Many people describe it as a "gentler" energy without the jitters or crash of coffee. On the tour, your guide explains the chemical differences in more detail.
  - q: Can we buy the "Mate Cup" (Gourd) there?
    a: Yes — the factory boutiques sell gourds and bombillas (the metal straws) alongside the yerba itself, from simple everyday ones to decorative pieces.
  - q: Is this a lot of driving for one day?
    a: Yes, honestly. Bella Vista is roughly 400km from Asunción, so a single-day trip means a long day on the road. Many guests pair it with our [Jesuit ruins tour](/jesuit-ruins-tour/) or read up on the [Jesuit missions](/jesuit-missions-paraguay/) region and split the drive over two days with an overnight stay.
  - q: Can I try it if I've never had it before?
    a: Of course — most visitors on this tour have never had real mate before. The tasting session is built for beginners, and your guide walks you through how to prepare and drink it properly.
  - q: Is the tour suitable for children?
    a: The educational part about nature is great, but the factory tour can be a bit long and technical for small kids. We'd recommend it more for teenagers or adults interested in agriculture or food science.
  - q: Do I need to speak Spanish?
    a: No. Your guide translates the technical agricultural Spanish used at the plantations and factories, so you won't need any Spanish of your own.
  - q: Is lunch included?
    a: Not in the tour price. Since this is a long day trip, we build in a stop at a local restaurant along the way, and you pay for your own meal there.
  - q: Can we see the harvest?
    a: Only during harvest season, roughly May to August, when you can see workers in the fields. Outside that window, the factories and drying houses are still open and running, just without the field activity.
