  ## See the Paraguay You Read About in Books

  Asunción is the brain of the country, but the "Golden Circuit" is its heart. This is where the red dust kicks up on colonial roads, where the legends of the Guaraní people are woven into fabric, and where the faith of a nation congregates.

itinerary:
  - title: "Stop 1: The City of Lace (Itauguá)"
    body: Our first stop is the home of Ñandutí (Spider Web), Paraguay's most famous textile. We don't just buy it; we visit the workshops. You will watch the nimble fingers of the weavers creating intricate geometric patterns that mimic the nature around them. You will hear the tragic and beautiful legend of how this lace was born.
  - title: "Stop 2: The Spiritual Capital (Caacupé)"
    body: We climb the hills to Caacupé, the home of the Virgin of Miracles. We enter the massive Basilica, a site of pilgrimage for millions. Even if you are not religious, the energy here is undeniable. We climb to the observation deck for a 360-degree view of the Cordillera hills—a perfect photo opportunity.
  - title: "Stop 3: The Creative Village (Areguá)"
    body: We finish in the charming colonial town of Areguá, perched on the shores of the legendary Lake Ypacaraí. Famous for its red clay soil, the entire town is an open-air art gallery of ceramics. We walk the historic "Avenida del Lago," admire the colonial mansions, and browse the endless colorful pottery stalls.

faq:
  - q: Is there a dress code for the Basilica?
    a: Yes. Caacupé is a functioning holy site. We recommend covering shoulders and knees out of respect (shorts are okay if they aren't too short, but tank tops are discouraged inside the church). It is good to bring a light scarf just in case.
  - q: Is lunch included in the price?
    a: No, the cost of the meal itself isn't included. We stop at a traditional restaurant along the route where you can order a full Paraguayan buffet or international dishes and pay directly. Let us know in advance about any dietary restrictions.
  - q: How much walking is involved?
    a: A moderate amount — walking the workshop rows in Itauguá, climbing to the Basilica's observation deck in Caacupé, and strolling the "Avenida del Lago" in Areguá. None of it is strenuous, but comfortable shoes help on the cobblestones and dirt paths.
  - q: Can I buy pottery and bring it home?
    a: Yes. Areguá's stalls sell everything from small pieces to larger ceramics, and we can help you pick items that pack well for a flight. For anything delicate or bulky, ask about padding or shipping when you book.
  - q: Is this tour suitable for children?
    a: Yes, families love this loop. The visual stimulation of the colorful pottery and the open spaces makes it kid-friendly. We can also add a stop for ice cream (Areguá is famous for strawberry desserts in season).
  - q: Do we visit the Lake Ypacaraí?
    a: You'll see it from the shore in Areguá along the "Avenida del Lago," but this loop doesn't include a boat ride. If you want more time on the water, our [San Bernardino trip](/san-bernardino-trip/) is built around the lake and pairs well as a second day.
  - q: Are the artisans working every day?
    a: Most workshops run Monday through Saturday, and many are family homes so hours vary. A few slow down on Sundays. Check with us when you book and we'll plan the route around who's working that day.
  - q: Is there a bathroom on the bus?
    a: The vehicle itself doesn't have a restroom, but we make stops in each town where you can use facilities at a shop, restaurant, or the Basilica in Caacupé.
