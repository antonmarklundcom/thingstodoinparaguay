San Bernardino sits on the eastern shore of Lake Ypacaraí in the Cordillera department, about an hour's drive from Asunción, and it has been Paraguay's go-to summer resort town for well over a century. Locals just call it "San Ber." What makes it different from most Paraguayan towns is its founding story: German and Swiss immigrants settled it in 1881, and their colonial-era houses, tree-lined streets, and orderly layout still give the town a distinct European feel that stands out from the rest of the Cordillera department.

## A brief history worth knowing

San Bernardino was founded in 1881 by a group of German and Swiss colonists — Santiago Schaerer is generally credited as the founding colony manager — and the town took its current name in honor of Bernardino Caballero, president of Paraguay at the time. That German heritage shows up today in surviving early-20th-century houses scattered around town, in a handful of German-influenced bakeries and restaurants, and in the general orderliness of the streets compared to the more organic layout of older Paraguayan towns.

## What to do in San Bernardino

The lakeside Costanera is the obvious starting point: a promenade along Lake Ypacaraí lined with restaurants, bars, and the sailing and rowing clubs that have operated here for decades. Renting a kayak or paddleboard, or just walking the Costanera at sunset when the light hits the water, is the simplest and most reliably good way to spend an afternoon. The town center itself rewards a slow walk — jacaranda trees line several streets and bloom purple in spring (roughly September to November), and a handful of surviving colonial-era buildings are worth spotting even without a formal tour.

Food-wise, look for "bollos," a local pastry San Bernardino is known for regionally — worth trying from more than one bakery, since recipes and quality vary. Beyond the town itself, Areguá — a smaller lakeside town famous for pottery and strawberries — sits a short drive around the lake and pairs naturally with a San Bernardino day trip if you have the extra time.

One honest note: Lake Ypacaraí has dealt with water-quality problems, including algae blooms, in various years, so it's worth checking current conditions before planning a swim specifically — the Costanera, restaurants, and town itself are worth the trip regardless of the lake's condition on any given day. If you have more than a day in the area, the pilgrimage town of [Caacupé](/caacupe/) is close enough to combine with a Cordillera loop.

## When to go

Summer (December through February, and into March) is peak season, when Asunción effectively empties out to its lake towns and San Bernardino gets genuinely lively — more restaurants open, the Costanera fills up in the evenings, and weekend traffic on the road out from Asunción can be heavy. Visiting outside peak summer means a quieter, more relaxed version of the same town, with lower prices at guesthouses and no crowds on the promenade, though some seasonal businesses may be closed — check ahead if a specific spot matters to your trip.

## Practical info for visiting San Bernardino

- **Getting there:** about 45–60 minutes by car or bus from Asunción, along Ruta 2 and then a short turn toward the lake; a private driver or organized day trip is the simplest option if you don't want to navigate bus routes
- **Best time to visit:** December–March for the full summer-resort atmosphere; April–November for a quieter, cooler visit with lower prices
- **What to bring:** sun protection, comfortable walking shoes for the Costanera, and swimwear if lake conditions look good on the day
- **Good pairing:** Areguá, a short drive around the lake, known for pottery workshops and strawberry stands
- **Where to stay:** guesthouses and small hotels cluster near the Costanera and town center — check current availability and prices before you go, since options shift season to season

## Frequently asked questions

### How far is San Bernardino from Asunción?
It's roughly 45–60 minutes by car under normal traffic, making it an easy half-day or full-day trip from the capital. Weekend traffic in peak summer can add extra time.

### Is Lake Ypacaraí safe to swim in?
Water quality has varied over the years due to periodic algae blooms, so it's worth checking current conditions locally before swimming rather than assuming. The town's other attractions — the Costanera, restaurants, and historic streets — are worth visiting regardless.

### What is San Bernardino known for?
It's Paraguay's most established summer lake resort, known for its German colonial history, lakeside Costanera, and role as the traditional escape from Asunción's summer heat since the late 19th century.

### Can I visit San Bernardino as a day trip, or should I stay overnight?
A day trip works well if you're mainly after the Costanera walk, a meal, and the historic streets. Staying overnight makes more sense in peak summer, when evenings on the lake are the main draw and traffic makes a same-day return less appealing.

### What else is nearby worth combining with a visit?
Areguá, a pottery and strawberry town on the other side of the lake, pairs naturally with San Bernardino for a fuller day trip around Lake Ypacaraí.

San Bernardino remains the easiest way to see a different, calmer side of [Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino,_Paraguay) without straying far from Asunción, and it works equally well as a quiet weekday walk or a full weekend in peak season.
